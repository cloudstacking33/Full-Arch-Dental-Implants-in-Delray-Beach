What a periodontist brings to implant care

A periodontist is a dental specialist trained in the structures that support your teeth, including the gums and jawbone. Periodontal knowledge helps us manage gum disease before it threatens an implant, rebuild support where bone has been lost, and place implants in positions that look natural and function well. It also helps when something has gone wrong. Many of the failed dental implant cases we see involve infection, poor positioning, or inadequate bone support, and a periodontist is well suited to diagnose those causes. Our implant dentistry combines surgical skill with a close eye on aesthetics, so your new teeth work well and look like they belong to you.

Frequently asked questions about dental implants

What are full arch dental implants?

Full arch dental implants replace all the teeth on the upper or lower jaw using several implants that support a fixed set of teeth. They are a popular alternative to removable dentures.

How long do dental implants last?

With good oral hygiene and regular checkups, many implants last for decades. Longevity depends on bone health, gum health, and daily care.

Can I get same day dental implants?

Many patients who have enough healthy bone and good overall health can receive teeth on the day of surgery. We confirm eligibility during your implant consultation.

What if my implant failed or I was told I do not have enough bone?

Failed implants can often be treated through dental implant rescue or revision, and limited bone may be addressed with grafting or zygomatic dental implants. An evaluation will clarify your options.

Does a periodontist place dental implants?

Yes. Periodontists are specialists in gums and bone, and placing implants is a central part of their training.

Dental Implants

Dental implants are small titanium posts placed in the jawbone to replace the roots of missing teeth. Once the implant bonds with the bone, a custom crown, bridge, or denture attaches to it, creating a replacement tooth that looks, feels, and functions like a natural one. Unlike removable options, implants do not slip, and they help preserve the jawbone that would otherwise shrink after tooth loss. They also protect neighboring teeth, since a bridge no longer requires grinding them down. At our Delray Beach office, we use implants to replace a single tooth, several teeth, or an entire arch. Each plan begins with an examination and imaging so we can place implants in the safest, most stable positions. Healing usually takes a few months, during which the implant integrates with the bone. Afterward, your restoration is fitted and adjusted for comfort and a natural bite. With good hygiene and regular checkups, dental implants can last for many years. Failed Dental Implant Treatment

A failed dental implant can be discouraging, but it is not the end of the road. Implants can fail for several reasons, including infection around the implant, insufficient bone, excess biting force, poor positioning, or underlying health conditions. Common signs include looseness, pain, swelling, bleeding gums, or a change in how the tooth feels. If you notice these symptoms, early evaluation matters, because quick action can sometimes save the implant or protect the surrounding bone. Our failed dental implant treatment begins with a careful diagnosis. We examine the area, take detailed images, and look for the cause so the problem does not repeat. Depending on what we find, treatment may include cleaning and treating infection, regenerating bone, removing the implant, or replacing it once the site has healed. As periodontal specialists, we are well suited to manage the gum and bone issues behind many failures. We explain your options clearly and move at a pace that protects healing. If you are worried about an implant that feels wrong, call our Delray Beach office promptly.

Dental Implant Rescue

Dental implant rescue is for patients whose implants are struggling and who want to save them before more damage occurs. Early signs include gum inflammation, bleeding when brushing, bone loss visible on X rays, or a feeling that the implant is not as solid as it was. Peri implantitis, an infection of the tissues around an implant, is a common culprit. Left untreated, it can destroy the bone that holds the implant in place. Rescue treatment focuses on stopping the infection, cleaning the implant surface, and rebuilding lost support whenever possible. Depending on severity, this may involve deep cleaning, medication, minor surgery, and bone or tissue grafting. We also look at contributing factors such as bite forces, home care habits, and restoration fit. Not every implant can be saved, but many can when treated early. At our Delray Beach practice, we are honest about prognosis and explain what is realistic. We also help you build habits that protect your implants going forward. Contact us if you notice changes around an implant.

Dental Implant Revision

Dental implant revision corrects problems with an implant or restoration that was placed previously, whether by our office or another provider. Reasons for revision include poor positioning, an implant that sits too deep or too shallow, a crown that looks unnatural, gum recession that exposes metal, or a bite that causes discomfort. Sometimes the existing implant can be adjusted or the restoration replaced. In other cases, the implant must be removed, the site healed and rebuilt, and a new implant placed. This is where fixing failed dental implants and correcting bad dental implants overlap with careful planning. We begin by reviewing your records and examining the area with detailed imaging. Then we explain what went wrong, what can be improved, and how long the process may take. Revision work demands precision, since you want the second attempt to succeed. Our periodontal background helps us manage gum aesthetics and bone support, which are often the heart of the problem. How to prepare for your consultation

A little preparation helps us make the most of your time. Bring a list of current medications, along with the name of your regular dentist or physician if you would like us to coordinate care. If you have recent dental X-rays, a previous implant record, or a treatment plan from another office, bring those as well. Write down your questions in advance, such as how long recovery takes, what the process involves, or how implants compare with dentures. Many patients also find it helpful to bring a family member or friend for support.

Questions before you call?

If you are unsure whether you need an appointment, call us. We are glad to answer general questions about dental implants, cosmetic dentistry, smile makeover treatment, and implant revision. If you are dealing with a painful or loose implant, tell us when you call so we can see you as soon as we reasonably can. Our team is here to help you move forward with clarity and confidence.
